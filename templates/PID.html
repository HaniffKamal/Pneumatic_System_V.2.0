The Brain: PID Control

Without control, a pneumatic system just pushes air blindly. A PID Controller acts like a brain, constantly checking where the piston is and adjusting the air to make it stop perfectly on target.

The "Driving" Analogy

The best way to understand PID is to imagine you are driving a car and trying to stay exactly in the center of your lane. The center line is your Target. Where your car is right now is your Current Position. The distance between them is the Error.

Proportional (P) "The Present"

If you are far away from the center line, you steer hard. If you are close, you steer gently. It reacts to your current mistake.

Integral (I) "The Past"

Imagine a strong crosswind is pushing you off the road. Even if you steer straight, you drift. The 'I' remembers this past drifting and holds the wheel slightly turned to fight the wind.

Derivative (D) "The Future"

If you are steering back towards the center line very fast, you will overshoot and cross into the other lane! The 'D' sees the future and slows down your steering before you cross the line.

The classic feedback loop: Measure the error, think (PID), and adjust the pressure.

Tuning the Brain

By adjusting how much P, I, and D we use (called "tuning"), we can make our pneumatic piston move perfectly. Here is what happens when you increase each one:

If you increase... Speed to Target Overshoot (Going too far) Stopping the Wind (Error)
Proportional (P) Faster Gets Worse Helps a little
Integral (I) Faster Gets Worse Fixes completely!
Derivative (D) No change Stops it completely! No change

That's why we use all three together! They balance each other out.

The Goal of a Good Brain
  • Be Fast: Reach the target line quickly.
  • Be Accurate: Don't leave any permanent gap between the car and the line (Zero Error).
  • Be Safe: Don't swerve wildly across the line (Minimize Overshoot).
