Simscape: The Real World

Simulink is like doing math on perfect paper. Simscape is like building a virtual prototype in a video game. It adds the "messiness" of the real world.

Virtual Prototyping

In Simscape, we don't just use math blocks. We connect virtual physical objects. Instead of a "Transfer Function", we actually drop a virtual air valve and a virtual pneumatic cylinder onto the screen.

The Virtual Cylinder

It acts just like real metal. It has weight, and the piston rubs against the walls (friction).

The Virtual Valve

It controls the air. It takes time to open and close, just like a real mechanical switch.

Inside the Virtual Machine

The Air Director (Valve)

This part acts like a traffic cop. It directs the high-pressure air (P) to push the piston one way, and lets the old air (T) escape out the back.

The Muscle (Actuator)

As air enters one side, it compresses (like a squishy spring) before it starts moving the heavy metal rod. This "squishiness" makes pneumatics very tricky to control in real life!

Math vs. Reality

Simulink (Perfect Math)

The blue line. It assumes air pushes instantly and perfectly. It stops exactly on target with 0% bounce.

Simscape (Messy Reality)

The red line. Because the air is squishy and the metal has momentum, it bounces past the target before stopping!

The "Real" Results

We used the exact same PID Brain as before, but this time on the virtual physical model.

Rise Time 0.50 s
Overshoot 29.05%
Settling Time 3.14 s
Wow! 29% Overshoot! Why? Because real air compresses like a balloon. The PID Brain was tuned for perfect math, so it pushed too hard in the messy real world. We need to re-tune the Brain for reality!
