Virtual Prototyping: Simulink

Before engineers build expensive machines in the real world, they test them on a computer. Simulink is like playing with digital Lego blocks to build a virtual machine.

What is Simulink?

Simulink is a famous computer program used by engineers all over the world. Instead of writing pages of complicated math equations, Simulink lets you drag and drop "blocks" onto a screen and connect them with wires.

The Lego Analogy: Imagine you want to build a toy car. In Simulink, you grab a "Motor Block", connect it to a "Wheel Block", and attach a "Battery Block" to see if it runs. It’s that visual!

Building Our Pneumatic Lego Set

To test our pneumatic piston, we just need three simple blocks connected together.

1. The Push (Step Block)

This acts like turning on the air compressor. It sends a burst of air pressure into the system.

2. The Machine (Transfer Fcn)

This holds the "recipe" we made on the Modeling page. It represents our Heavy Block, Sticky Floor, and Rubber Band.

3. The Screen (Scope Block)

This is just a computer monitor block that draws a graph so we can see the piston moving.

Inside the Machine Block: We just type in our numbers for Mass, Friction, and the Spring, and the computer does the rest!

The Result (No Brain Yet)

When we hit the "Play" button, the Scope Block shows us this graph. Because we haven't added a PID "Brain" yet, the piston just shoots out blindly when the air hits it.

Report Card

How did our "Brainless" system do?

Rise Time (How fast?) 0.93 s
Settling Time (When does it stop wiggling?) 2.69 s
Overshoot (Did it go too far?) 5.83 %
Final Value 0.065 m
Conclusion: It works, but it bounces too much. It needs a controller!
